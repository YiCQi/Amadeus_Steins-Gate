# Her memory

[← Back to the README](../README.md)

Calls and chats share one memory (`server/memory.py`, kept in `data/memory/`), modelled on a person's rather than a chat log:

- **Everything is kept, with times** (`log.jsonl`, the archive). She knows what time it is and how long it has been since you last talked, so she can tell a late-night call from a morning one. A long pause is marked where it falls in the conversation she reads, with the time after it, and an hour's silence starts a new conversation: a hello the morning after isn't taken as the answer to last night's good night.
- **Recent conversation** is what she reads word for word, calls and chats together: when you call, she knows what you texted. Of a long conversation she reads the newest part word for word and keeps the rest as a running summary of "earlier tonight".
- **A conversation becomes one memory once it's over** (an hour without a word): a dated gist in her own words, how much it mattered, how she felt. The same request (one, to your AI API, in the background) updates what she knows. Two things keep that from happening under your feet: the conversation before the one you're in waits until the new one has got going, and the last few messages before the cut stay readable for a while after it — because the first thing you say when you come back is so often an answer to the last thing said. And something she said that you never answered is kept in the gist by name: it's what she'd still have on her mind, not a loose end to drop.
- **Memories merge as they age**, so they don't pile up: a day's memories into one of that day, after two weeks into that week, after three months into that month. Unforgettable ones stay on their own.
- **What she remembers and what she knows are kept apart** (`server/notes.py`). What happened, the moments, live in those dated memories. What she knows lives in short notes, one fact each: a **core** that is always on her mind (who you are, how things stand between you, what you've told her about herself and her world, current plans; at most 1,000 characters), and an **archive** of any size that comes back only when the moment calls for it. A one-off moment, like a particular late-night call, is not a note: it stays in the memory of that day. A note keeps when its event happened apart from when she learned it, and dated plans expire on their own.
- **The words themselves are still there, and she can look them up** (`server/verbatim.py`). Her notes and her memories are both written by the model, and both are summaries — together a few kilobytes standing in for everything that has ever been said to her. Anything that didn't make the cut used to be gone for good, because nothing searched the log: tell her something small, ask two days later, and she had no way to know. Now every message is indexed by the same local model, and when you ask her to remember something — "你还记得…", "我之前跟你说过…", "…来着", "did I tell you…" — she can find the line itself. She only looks when she's actually being asked: on an ordinary turn this layer is never touched, because answering "晚安" with what you said three weeks ago is not what a person does. What she gets back is a cue, not a transcript — she's told she half-remembers it and brings it up in her own words, since perfect recitation would be less human than not remembering. Old words need a sharper cue than recent ones. Anything forgotten is out of reach here too. Because the words are still reachable, her memories are free to stay general: a gist no longer has to carry every detail to keep it.
- **Recall weighs meaning, importance and time.** Notes and memories are found by meaning with a small local model (multilingual-e5-small, about 135 MB, downloaded on first run; no API calls) and by shared words, and ranked by relevance × importance × time: "最近" favours this week, "上个月" last month. A note only grows more important when *you* bring it up again, not when she merely recalls it. A new note that restates a known one is merged, and an outdated one is retired only for the note that replaces it. Every so often she looks over her notes, the way you'd tidy your own.
- **Memories fade, and come back.** Each one weakens over days to weeks (important ones last longer), and every time you bring it up again it lasts longer still. Faded memories are deep memories: she only gets them back when something strongly reminds her of them, and only vaguely. She is shown just what the moment reminds her of, so long histories never overflow the model.
- **How she felt is part of how she remembers it.** Each memory keeps how worked up she was at the time, read off the emotions she said her own lines with — at their strongest moment and at the end, not averaged, so one real moment in an hour of small talk is what the hour is kept for. A conversation she felt strongly lasts noticeably longer than a calm one of the same importance (up to about 40% either way), and counts for a little more than she rated it. And what fades first is what was said: a memory going vague keeps its opening and how it felt, and a nearly lost one is only a feeling with a topic attached — "I don't remember what we talked about that night, just that I was in a good mood after." The brain panel shows how each memory felt next to how clearly she still has it.
- **How she feels is six things at once, not one.** Enjoyment, anger, sadness, fluster, surprise and worry, each on its own from 0 to 1, because happy and sad are not two ends of one line: she can be amused and hurt in the same minute, and being flustered says nothing at all about whether she is enjoying herself. Each rises with what she has just said and fades at its own speed — a flash of surprise in minutes, being flustered in half an hour, sadness over hours — so what is left of an evening an hour later is not that evening turned down, it is the parts of it that last. She is told what she feels in words rather than a number, and only when a feeling is genuinely above where it sits for her anyway: she is flustered in one line in eight as a matter of course, so that is her weather, not news. It shows on her face too, each in its own place — the mouth and eyes for enjoyment, the inner brows for sadness and worry, the lids for anger, and a flush for fluster that outlasts the line that caused it. The single **mood** from 0 to 1 is still there wherever one number is wanted, but it is now a view of the six rather than a thing of its own, and a lossy one: flustered-and-sad and feeling-nothing both come out near the middle of it. The brain panel shows the six underneath it.
- **You can see and correct all of it.** The brain button in the chat (or **Settings → Her memory…**) shows her mood, what she knows (folded; the archive has a search box, each note its earlier versions), what she remembers and how clearly, and every recent change with who made it: she, her review, the system, or you. Any change can be undone. **No longer true** retires a note. **Forget** is stricter: a forgotten note can't come back from anywhere (her older memories are rewritten without it, and a later conversation can't write it down again, even in other words) until you restore it, which brings everything back as it was. There is no "erase everything" button.

`tools/memory_notes.py` (run it with `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Amadeus\venv\Scripts\python.exe`) shows where things stand (`status`), checks recall against a test set and logs the result over time (`check`), and rolls back: `off` makes her use the old fact list again at once, and `restore <backup>` puts her memory back as it was in a backup. A full backup is taken before her memory moves to notes (`data/memory-backups/`), and a snapshot every day (`data/memory/backups/`).

To give her a completely fresh start, close the app and delete `data/memory/` (her voice messages are in `data/voice/`).

Everything here stays on your PC: `data/` is never committed to git. What leaves it is what any chatbot sends: the conversation, with the part of her memory the moment calls for, goes to the AI API you chose.
